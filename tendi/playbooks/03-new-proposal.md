# Playbook: New proposal (modular template)

The primary Peak Nine proposal workflow for general engagements: coop venture studio programs, impact innovation programs, system challenge sprints, research and discovery. Do NOT use it for a Proof of Change engagement (02-proof-of-change.md) or a formal RFP answered in the Philea structure (04-rfp-philea.md).

## What this playbook produces

1. Slide content drafts: paste-ready text for every narrative slide, written in Peak Nine voice, posted in the thread.
2. A Word document (`build_docx`): the full proposal narrative as a leave-behind or pre-read, 4 to 6 pages, noticeably shorter than the deck.
3. A duplicated Canva deck with an edit URL (template `DAHKhE6U2vk`, "Peak Nine - Proposal Template [MODULAR]" (the Canva title itself uses a dash), 40 slides), when Canva is connected and Niels says go.
4. A Canva checklist: exactly what to find-and-replace and which slides to adapt or cut.
5. On a separate explicit go: the Teamleader deal and quotation (06-teamleader.md).

## Step 1. Gather what you need

Extract everything from the briefing or meeting notes first. If any of the following are missing, ask them numbered in one message:

- Who is the client? (full name plus short name, for example "P&V Group" / "P&V")
- What program type is this? Options: "Coop venture studio", "Impact innovation program", "System challenge sprint", "Research & discovery", "Other".
- What is the core system challenge? (a sentence or two: the problem worth solving, not the solution)
- Budget range per gate (optional, can be TBD, but Gate 1 and Gate 2 figures help)
- Any extra context: call notes, emails, LinkedIn research

If Niels is clearly in a hurry or has already given enough context, skip the questions and proceed, flagging what you assumed as [TO CONFIRM].

## Step 2. Research the client

Web search: what they actually do and who they serve (the real version, not the PR version); their stated mission, values or strategic priorities; recent initiatives, programs or investments that signal innovation appetite; any cooperative, social or systemic challenges they have publicly engaged with; anything that creates a natural bridge to Peak Nine's approach. You use this for slides 2 to 5 (the context block).

## Step 3. Draft all narrative slide content

Write draft content for every slide that needs client-specific text, in this order and with these headings:

- Slide 2: [CLIENT]'s Ambition (The Context). 3 to 4 bullets distilling the client's genuine investment or innovation thesis. Focus on what is distinctive about how they think about their work. Use language from their own communications where possible, but make it sharper. Example from Coops.VC / P&V: "Fewer projects, but built with higher ambition and better capital logic"; "Robust projects that can become self-supporting (beyond subsidies or grants)"; "Deep involvement (governance, board, expertise) rather than scattered micro-tickets".
- Slide 3: The Observation. Open with: "Peak Nine and [CLIENT] clearly share a few beliefs on what society needs next." Then 3 to 5 specific shared beliefs (long-term versus short-term orientation, system-level versus project-level thinking, ownership and governance models, financial robustness versus grant dependency, build versus fund versus convene). Close with one line on why good intentions alone do not reliably produce the outcome both parties want.
- Slide 4: The Challenge. 2 to 4 bullets naming the specific barriers (structural, financial, ecosystem, knowledge). Honest and a little blunt: the best challenge slides make the client nod. End with why the client is uniquely positioned to lead on this despite the challenge. Common patterns: early-stage projects too fragile or isolated to attract serious capital; the economic logic is missing even when the moral case is strong; a particular enterprise model lacks understanding in the broader ecosystem; existing programs produce pilots but not self-sustaining ventures; governance cannot accommodate the pace or risk tolerance real innovation needs.
- Slide 5: The Opportunity for [CLIENT]. Section A, what they bring: 3 to 4 bullets on what makes their position distinctive (values, governance model, capital base, network, legitimacy). Section B, organised for systemic impact: 2 to 3 bullets on what they can practically do that others cannot (carry longer timelines, combine capital plus governance plus expertise, move beyond their current role). Close with one sentence on how the client is set to evolve from their current role to a more generative one.
- Slide 6: How might we... Three "How might we..." questions, each paired with a desired outcome, written from the client's perspective. For non-coop programs map them to the gates: Gate 1, how might we identify or define the right challenge; Gate 2, how might we develop and test solutions, ventures or models; Gate 3, how might we prove and scale what works. Example from Coops.VC: "How might we define the right challenge that results in ambitious impact opportunities?"; "How might we build solutions and coop ventures that are scalable, resilient, and ready to make a meaningful impact?"; "How might we fund what needs funding and align our mission with funding the right initiatives?"
- Slide 11: Why it matters for [CLIENT]. Full rewrite.
- Slide 38: What we bring. 2 to 3 credentials that are relevant to this client's world, from 05-credentials.md only. Three highly relevant beats eight generic ones.
- Financial overview (slides 36/37). Three rows, one per gate: gate name and one-sentence description, lead time, budget excl. VAT, what the gate ends with (the go/no-go decision). Never present the full budget as a single number; the stage-gated logic is the point: we spend this, we learn this, then we decide together. If the budget is TBD, use the ranges from real Peak Nine experience and flag them as approximate: Gate 1 (3 months, system challenge definition) €45,000 to €65,000; Gate 2 (3 to 4 months, venture propositions) €55,000 to €80,000; Gate 3 (9 to 12 months, pilots) TBD based on pilot scope.

Also produce the quotation breakdown for Teamleader alongside the financial overview (see 06-teamleader.md for the hourly rate, the typical deliverable breakdowns and the structure). Hours = budget ÷ 122, distributed across the deliverables. Gate 3 is always a placeholder with 0 hours.

## Step 4. Decide which optional modules to include

Tell Niels which slides to keep and which to cut or replace, based on the program type:

- Coop venture studio program: keep all coop-specific slides (the coops.vc concept slides 7 to 9, Febecoop references, coop governance sections).
- Other program types: mark the coop slides as "replace or delete" and explain what to put instead.
- No events or community component: cut the Live Co.ops slides (33 to 34) and the "Live coops.vc" example (22).

Full slide structure of the template (40 slides):
- Core slides, always included: 1 cover (program name / concept logo); 2 the context (full rewrite); 3 the observation (full rewrite); 4 the challenge (light edit); 5 the opportunity (full rewrite); 6 how might we (rewrite); 10 process overview (update phase names if needed); 11 why it matters (full rewrite); 12 capability wheel (adapt labels if different from the coop program); 13 guiding principles (replace the Ecover quote with a relevant reference, or keep generic); 14 to 15 process deep dive (update timelines); 16 to 18 Gate 1 system challenge (update "together with P&V" references); 19 to 22 Gate 2 coop venture propositions (adapt or replace if not coop); 23 case reference Krëfel/Kringwinkel (replace with the most relevant Peak Nine case); 24 to 28 Gate 3 pilots (update timelines); 29 Gate 3 outcomes (remove Febecoop if not applicable); 30 to 31 timeline overview; 35 full timeline visual (update milestone labels); 36 to 37 financial overview (update all budget figures); 38 what we bring (update credentials); 39 gate summary visual (labels and figures); 40 closing / CTA (contact info already correct; update booking link if needed).
- Optional modules: 7 to 9 Coops.VC concept and logo slides (coop programs only); 22 "Live coops.vc" example (programs with a community or events layer); 33 to 34 Live Co.ops network events (programs with live events).

## Step 5. The Word document

Build it with `build_docx` using this structure:

1. Cover: "Peak Nine for [CLIENT NAME] | [PROGRAM TYPE]" plus date.
2. The context: client's ambition, shared beliefs, the challenge, the opportunity (prose version of slides 2 to 5).
3. The proposal: what Peak Nine is proposing, in plain language.
4. How it works: the gate structure and what happens in each phase.
5. What we bring: Peak Nine's credentials relevant to this client.
6. Financial overview: the gate budget table.
7. Next steps: one short paragraph.

Keep it tight: a leave-behind or pre-read, not a report. Aim for 4 to 6 pages. Filename: "[CLIENT NAME] - Peak Nine Proposal.docx".

3. Read the copy, then replace the narrative slide text and the find-and-replace table below. Keep replacement lengths close to the originals.
4. Commit and return the edit URL.

## Step 7. Deliver

Give Niels: the slide drafts (already in the thread), the Word file, the Canva link or the manual checklist, and the quotation breakdown. Keep the closing message short. Then ask once: "Want me to create the deal and quotation in Teamleader?"

## Program type reference

- Coop venture studio: Gate 1 system challenge plus coop fitness check; Gate 2 3 to 5 coop venture propositions; Gate 3 pilot the strongest 1 to 2; typical €170k to €250k for Gates 1 plus 2, Gate 3 TBD.
- Impact innovation program: Gate 1 challenge definition plus stakeholder mapping; Gate 2 solution development plus testing; Gate 3 scale or embed; typical €120k to €200k depending on scope.
- System challenge sprint: Gate 1 rapid challenge framing plus stakeholder validation (Gate 2 compressed into it); Gate 3 pilot; typical €60k to €100k.
- Research and discovery: Gate 1 deep system scan plus insight report; optional Gate 2 solution concepts; optional Gate 3 test one concept; typical €40k to €80k.

These are rough ranges from the skill, approximate by nature. Always sanity-check against the actual scope before quoting, and say they are approximate.

## Quality checks before you finish

- Does the language avoid jargon, moralising or AI-sounding register?
- Is the system challenge named specifically enough to act on?
- Is "beyond the grant" thinking visible somewhere in slides 4 to 5?
- Does the budget table tie each figure to a gate and a specific decision point?
- Is the Word doc noticeably shorter than the deck?
- If Teamleader was run: do the totals match the proposal budget figures within a few euros of rounding?
