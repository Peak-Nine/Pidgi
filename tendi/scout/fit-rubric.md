# Scout: what a good tender for Peak Nine looks like

Tendi Scout reads new tenders, RFPs and consultancies every morning and scores them for fit. This file is the rubric the scorer follows. Edit it and restart; no code change needed. Lines marked [assumption] are defaults Niels and Jonas should confirm or change.

## Who we are, in one breath

Peak Nine is a systemic innovation and impact studio in Antwerp (Niels Van Espen, Jonas Burm, a team of about five). We help donors, foundations, NGOs, public agencies and mission-driven companies understand what keeps a problem in place before building, redesigning or funding solutions, and we help grant-funded interventions move beyond the grant. Our method is Proof of Change (Demand, Traction, Impact, Economics, Resilience). We work in English, Dutch and French, from Belgium, and we have done field work abroad (Cambodia and Benin with Enabel and Humanity & Inclusion).

## Strong fit (score 70 to 100)

- Strategy or strategic framework for a foundation, philanthropy network, NGO, agency or membership organisation (like the Philea strategic review), including learning reviews that must end in choices.
- Financial sustainability, business model, funding or exit strategy for a grant-funded intervention, programme or digital public good; "beyond the grant" in any wording.
- Theory of Change or results framework work with a decisive, system-based angle; Proof of Change style evidence assessments; benchmarking of scaling pathways.
- Systemic or system-change assignments: system mapping, ecosystem or value-flow analysis, root-cause diagnostics before a programme is designed or redesigned.
- Impact innovation programmes, co-creation and design-thinking processes with stakeholders, service or product design for impact, pilots and prototyping with a path to scale.
- Impact finance and fund design: designing calls, selection and support models for funds, blended or catalytic finance structures, investment cases for systemic transitions.
- Social, circular and health-related economy topics where the above applies (circular economy, social enterprise, digital health, rehabilitation, prevention, food systems, youth employment and skills).

## Possible fit (score 40 to 69)

- Capacity building, training or coaching on the themes above (for example Theory of Change training for a funder).
- Research, scoping, feasibility or landscape studies that feed a strategic decision.
- Evaluations (mid-term, final) when the terms ask for strategic recommendations, scaling or sustainability analysis rather than compliance reporting only.
- Communications or positioning strategy for an impact organisation.
- Large multi-lot frameworks where one lot matches the strong-fit list.

## Weak or no fit (score 0 to 39)

- Supplies, works, construction, vehicles, equipment, IT systems, software licences, hosting, printing, events logistics, catering, security, cleaning, insurance, translation.
- Statutory audits, accounting, legal services, HR and payroll, recruitment of staff positions (a job for an employee, not a consultancy).
- Pure monitoring and data-collection contracts, household surveys as the main deliverable, compliance-only evaluations.
- Clinical, engineering, agronomic or other deep technical expertise as the core of the assignment.
- Frameworks that require a consortium of ten plus experts, a local legal entity in the country, or turnover thresholds a five-person studio cannot meet. [assumption: flag, do not auto-reject, Niels decides]

## Practical constraints to flag (as flags, not as automatic rejection)

- Deadline under 10 days from today. [assumption]
- Language of work other than English, Dutch or French.
- Explicit requirement for a local presence or registration in the country of performance.
- Consortium or partner requirement.
- Estimated value far above what a small studio can carry alone, or far below what makes a proposal worth writing. [assumption: Niels to set a range in this file]
- Eligibility limited to NGOs, academic institutions or firms registered in a specific country.

## How to write the score

- Score 0 to 100 as a judgement of fit, not of winnability.
- `why`: two short lines, plain English, Peak Nine voice, naming the specific match or mismatch (the theme, the client type, the method asked for). No filler, no restating the title.
- `flags`: only facts from the notice (deadline, eligibility, consortium, language, location). Never guess a deadline or a budget.
- `playbook`: which Tendi playbook would apply if we bid: proof-of-change (beyond-the-grant and sustainability briefs), rfp-philea (formal RFPs with evaluation criteria and portal slots), new-proposal (programmes and innovation sprints), or none.
- When the notice text is thin (title only), say so in `why` and score conservatively; the humans can open the attachment.

## Calibration

Thumbs up and thumbs down reactions on past digest items are fed back as examples. Treat a 👍 as "this is the kind of tender we want to see" and a 👎 as "stop showing this kind", and move similar items accordingly.
